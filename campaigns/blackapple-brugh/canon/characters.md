# Characters, factions, voices, and relationships

The descriptions below are authoring anchors. They are not character stat
blocks. The fifth-srd conversion supplies level, class, checks, conditions, and
combat values when a runtime definition needs them.

| `faction.blackapple-villagers` | Provincial farmers, innkeepers, merchants, and families | Keep the village safe and prosperous without provoking the forest | They distrust strangers until the party helps someone. They fear both fairy magic and public shame. |

## Principal court and family NPCs

### `npc.elf-lord` — the Mirror Man

**Source fact:** The Elf Lord is an ancient Brugh Elf confined to the Brugh by
old magic. He presents himself as a noble in dark green, speaks an old form of
Elvish through Moth-in-Water, understands Common and modern Elvish, and changes
his opinion of visitors quickly. He watches arrivals through magic and may
order servants to use the pit.

**Voice:** Formal, antique, and precise when naming court ritual; suddenly
petulant about an animal genealogy, an insult, or a remembered battle. He
never explains a demand twice. His courtesy sounds like a threat because he
assumes the party already belongs to his audience.

**Goal:** Keep the Brugh as a private court, preserve the illusion of his old
power, and turn intruders into entertainment, servants, or an interesting
bargain.

**Secret:** The old accommodation and the prison magic leave him powerful but
isolated. The children are servants because he treats people as movable pieces,
not because the court needs their labor. He is afraid of an empty court and
will trade more than he admits if offered a way to keep his story intact.

**Relationships:** Moth-in-Water is his trusted lieutenant and translator;
Master Ned is a former war steed he humiliates; the children are charmed
servants; the ibix are disposable allies. Wylda fascinates him because she
knows the mirror without being invited.

**Adaptation rule:** He can be evaded, deceived, negotiated with, or opposed.
His death is one possible event outcome, never a required victory condition.

### `npc.moth-in-water` — jester and majordomo

**Source fact:** Moth-in-Water has served the Elf Lord for centuries. He is
boisterous, rude, speaks about himself in the third person, juggles sharp
objects, and translates the Elf Lord's ancient speech. He is almost always with
the Elf Lord except in his own chamber.

**Voice:** Loud third-person self-reference, fake announcements, and jokes that
turn into a practical warning. He fills silence before anyone else can use it.

**Goal:** Keep the court entertaining and himself indispensable. He prefers a
clever audience to a dead one because corpses cannot applaud.

**Secret:** He understands that the Elf Lord's moods are dangerous and quietly
keeps routes open for his own survival. He may translate a threat less harshly
if the party treats him as a person rather than furniture.

**Relationships:** Loyal to the Elf Lord but not blindly; aware of every
child's work station; contemptuous of the ibix's sloppy manners; grudgingly
amused by Wylda and Master Ned.

### `npc.wylda-figwort` — heir who wants the road

**Source fact:** Wylda is nineteen, the oldest Figwort child, a romantic and
self-described adventurer. She is the only household member who knows the
drawing-room mirror is a portal and wants to see the other side. Her theft
skills come from longstanding kleptomania.

**Voice:** Quick, conspiratorial, and self-aware about the difference between a
romantic story and a bad plan. She asks questions about every companion's past.

**Goal:** Learn the truth about the mirror, rescue her brother, and make one
choice for herself before the household chooses for her.

**Secret:** She has held the mirror knowledge back because she fears being
treated as a child or locked away. She also knows enough family politics to
predict which parent will ask the party for a private favor.

**Relationships:** Loves Arthur, is wary of Lady Figwort's control, and thinks
Lord Figwort is harmless only when someone competent is nearby. She can recruit
into the party before entry; she leaves if the party uses children as bait.

### `npc.lord-figwort` — title and costume

**Source fact:** Lord Figwort is a foppish dandy whose main passion is fashion;
he lets his wife and Pigman Jack handle village administration. He knows Arthur
is not himself but does not know about the Brugh.

**Voice:** Polite, self-important, and eager to discuss clothing, status, or
the correct public version of an embarrassing event.

**Goal:** Preserve the family title and avoid a scandal involving his son. He
will pay for Arthur's safe return if the party gives him a story he can repeat.

**Secret:** He is more frightened of losing authority than of supernatural
truth. If shown a credible plan, he can authorize access and money; if publicly
humiliated, he calls for soldiers.

### `npc.lady-figwort` — household protector

**Source fact:** Lady Figwort is tall and thin, watches strangers closely, and
opposes Dr. Livinius operating on Arthur. She may quietly hire the party to
remove “Arthur” from the sanitarium, even by force.

**Voice:** Low, controlled, and practical. She asks what will happen to the
child after the impressive part of a plan.

**Goal:** Protect her children and keep her household from public disgrace.

**Secret:** Her certainty that Arthur is wrong is stronger than her evidence;
she is willing to endanger another child to restore the family image unless the
party confronts that contradiction.

**Relationships:** Conflicts with Lord Figwort's appetite for appearances and
with Livinius's confidence. She respects a party that refuses unnecessary harm.

### `npc.dr-livinius` — healer of bad ideas

**Source fact:** Livinius runs the sanitarium, studies fairy lore, and has
diagnosed the ibix posing as Arthur with a fictional category of demonic
imbalance. He proposes brain surgery and knows general local fairy history but
not the active manor portal.

**Voice:** Soft, clinical, and overconfident; he explains a dangerous idea as
though it were a household repair.

**Goal:** Prove his theory and gain access to a real fairy phenomenon for
research.

**Secret:** He is not malicious in his own estimation, but he values an
interesting case above the patient's consent. His fairy expertise can identify
the shape-change and the significance of mirrors if the party stops the
operation first.

**Adaptation rule:** Surgery is an imminent harm and a choice to prevent. The
campaign does not make institutional abuse a required spectacle or reward.

### `npc.flynn` — keeper of the Hen's Teeth

**Source fact:** Flynn is a half-elf adopted by the Jolly Fox owners. He runs
the forest tavern, talks readily about local news, keeps a loaded crossbow
behind the bar, and knows the portal but does not reveal it to strangers.

**Voice:** Mild, slow, and conversational, with a long pause before a useful
answer. He dislikes arguments more than he fears fairies.

**Goal:** Keep the tavern safe and the old balance undisturbed while protecting
customers who respect his house.

**Secret:** He has seen the midnight mirror open and knows Periwinkle is not an
ordinary traveler. He will help a party that protects his patrons and keeps the
mirror secret until it is safe.

### `npc.periwinkle-black` — midnight witness

**Source fact:** Periwinkle Black is a Brugh Elf magic-user/thief who arrives at
the Hen's Teeth, smokes a hookah, answers in brief phrases, and enters the
mirror at midnight.

**Voice:** Short, noncommittal, and impossible to rush. She answers the question
behind a question only when the party stops trying to impress her.

**Goal:** Observe the surface world and report what changes around the portal.

**Secret:** She is testing whether the Elf Lord's court still has a future. She
can become an anonymous source of a route clue, but she never promises loyalty.

### `npc.master-ned` — talking war horse

**Source fact:** Master Ned is a speaking horse imprisoned in the Brugh's
dungeon for looking at the Elf Lord “sideways.” He is tired of the court and
willing to follow people who treat him well.

**Voice:** Dry, literal, and offended by noble euphemisms. He remembers routes
through the Brugh better than he remembers court etiquette.

**Goal:** Leave the Brugh and choose his own companions.

**Secret:** He knows the Elf Lord's routines and can reveal that the apparent
torture chamber is a punishment theater. He fears being left behind more than he
fears the court.

**Adaptation rule:** If rescued, Ned is an optional travel companion and witness,
not a replacement for a player character or a free combat advantage.

## Village, forest, and service NPCs

### `npc.agatha` — apothecary

Agatha sells practical and magical-like potions, including sleep tea, healing
rain, and powders with unpredictable side effects. She speaks cheerfully while
checking whether customers understand a label. Her goal is to keep the village
supplied; her secret is that her experiments are less reliable than her sales
pitch. She can provide a noncombat recovery route and a clue about the poisoned
poet.

### `npc.ms-goodall` — appraiser and mother

Ms. Goodall runs the gem and treasure shop, appraises magic discreetly, and is
the mother of Amelia and Bernard. She is a retired spellcaster with a practical
eye for value. Her voice is brisk and transactional until the subject turns to
her children. She wants a quiet rescue, fears public embarrassment, and can
provide a reward only for children actually returned. She suspects Tobler's
past, which connects the shop to the cemetery investigation.

### `npc.tobler` — taxidermist with a second trade

Tobler presents as a retired city watch captain who buys and preserves animal
corpses. His military manner and bushy mustache disguise a necromancer who
animates small specimens. He wants rare bodies and a quiet place to work. His
secret gives the party a morally messy cemetery lead: exposing him can protect
the dead but also destabilize village trust if done carelessly.

### `npc.pigman-jack` — farmer and wereboar

Jack is the village's capable day-to-day organizer and a pig farmer who can
point to the wild-dog lair. The source makes his infection a surprise threat;
the adaptation signals that he has missing time and keeps a recovery route open
so the party can help him rather than treat him as a disposable ambush. He
speaks plainly, wants the farms safe, and is embarrassed by the condition.

### `npc.billy-blurtweed` — Faehill brownie

Billy watches from invisibility or animal shapes, mocks overconfident travelers,
and lives in a small stone shelter among the fairy ruins. His voice is a
disembodied stage whisper followed by a laugh. He wants the hill left alone and
secretly enjoys seeing whether outsiders can learn. Respect earns a warning
about digging; greed earns a false direction or a curse-themed setback.

### `npc.smithson` — the marked traveler

Smithson was cursed after trying to dig into the Faehill. He rings a bell and
warns others away. The adaptation treats his visible disease as a condition
deserving distance, care, and dignity; it does not use contagion as a surprise
gotcha. He wants someone to believe his warning and can describe the mound's
danger.

### `npc.father-naskew` — quiet priory caretaker

Father Naskew leaves food for Smithson and mediates between villagers and the
priory. His voice is tired but kind. He wants calm without concealment and can
provide a safe place to recover a frightened child or contain an exposed
double.

### `npc.cu-sidhe` and `npc.shrine-owl` — forest voices

The cu-sidhe guards the Faehill and communicates through bark, posture, and the
behavior of local dogs. The shrine owl demands a confession before its cure.
Both are adaptation opportunities for truth and consent: the party can offer a
fictional travel confession, decline and leave, or earn help by respecting the
forest. Neither requires a player to disclose personal real-world information.

## The seven children and their doubles

The children are frightened, charmed, and noncombatant by default. A gold
thread at the left ankle is the recurring rescue clue. Each child gets one
personal memory or object in the Brugh so the rescue is a person, not a counter.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `child.arthur-figwort` | Eleven, proud and ashamed; tries to sound like a lord | Guards the wine cellar. He recognizes Wylda's private nickname after the thread is removed. | `double.arthur-figwort` is the loudest and most destructive; it was moved to the sanitarium and can be exposed through its mask or impossible memory. |
| `child.amelia-goodall` | Twelve, observant and protective of Bernard | Serves at dinner. She remembers an inventory code from her mother's shop. | `double.amelia-goodall` uses poisonous mushrooms and tries to isolate witnesses. |
| `child.bernard-goodall` | Ten, nervous, practical, carries a remembered sling | Serves at dinner. The sling in C16 is a tangible confirmation. | `double.bernard-goodall` imitates obedience but cannot complete a familiar family counting game. |
| `child.giles-weadley` | Twelve, curious, asks about every fungus | Tends the bluehouse. Lingonberry liqueur or the mushroom ambassador can help identify him. | `double.giles-weadley` avoids the forest's smell and becomes agitated around dead wood. |
| `child.philip-anvil` | Ten, eager to be useful, repeats service phrases | Waits in C14. He remembers a smith's rhythm when freed. | `double.philip-anvil` makes needless “helpful” suggestions that trap adults in chores. |
| `child.ursula-cooke` | Five, timid, loves rhythm and a cloth doll | Plays timpani in C12a. The doll in C16 and a simple beat confirm her identity. | `double.ursula-cooke` uses noise and mimicry to attract adults, but cannot keep time with the village bell. |

### Ibix rules of portrayal

The doubles are antagonists because they choose harm and concealment, not
because they are goatfolk. A player may expose a double through testimony,
objects, a mirror, a fairy witness, or a controlled confrontation. The campaign
does not require killing a child-shaped figure in front of the village. If a
double is killed, its true form and mask are evidence, but the corresponding
child remains captive until separately rescued.
